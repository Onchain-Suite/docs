title: "Contract-Based Flows"
description: "React to normalized smart contract activity, configure on-chain triggers that fire on mints, swaps, approvals, and liquidations."
---

Contract-based flows respond to what wallets actually do on-chain: minting, swapping, borrowing, approving, getting liquidated. For **EVM contracts, the platform watches the chain for you**, publish an automation with an on-chain trigger and it starts firing on real activity, no indexer to run and no ABI to paste.

## Scenarios worth building

| Moment | Trigger | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First mint | `holder_acquired` | Welcome series |
| Approval granted, no swap | `approval_intent` | Wait, then nudge |
| Tokens moved to an exchange | `exchange_outflow` | Retention message |
| Position liquidated | `liquidation_detected` | Recovery guidance |
| Liquidity withdrawn | `capital_withdrawn` | Win-back |
| Proposal opened | `governance_activity` | Voting reminder |

## How it fires

Give the trigger a **chain and a contract address**, then publish. That's it: no ABI to paste and no indexer to run. Matching on-chain activity starts creating automation entries, after a short finality delay so a re-orged transaction never messages anyone (seconds on an L2, a couple of minutes on Ethereum mainnet).

<Warning>
**Automatic watching is EVM-only.** For Solana contracts, or if you run your own indexer, send events in through the [Automation Trigger API](/api/automation-triggers) instead.
</Warning>

<Note>
If the contract isn't verified publicly, paste its ABI (or just the one event's signature) when you build the trigger. Until a trigger has a resolvable event, publishing reports it as skipped rather than doing nothing silently.
</Note>

## Configuring the trigger

In the builder, a **preset needs one input, your token or contract**. The event and filters that define the moment are filled in for you, so you pick the contract and publish. See [pick your token, done](/automation/triggers-and-conditions#business-presets-pick-your-token-done). Adding a contract address scopes the trigger to your protocol; leaving it empty matches the moment on **any** contract on the chain.

Under the hood, that same trigger is a small piece of JSON, which is also what you'd set directly when building over the API. For a preset the `eventNames` and `filters` are seeded from the preset; you don't type them in the builder:

```json
{
  "type": "swap_completed",
  "chain": "base-mainnet",
  "contractAddress": "0xYourPool",
  "eventNames": ["Swap", "TokenExchange"],
  "filters": [
    { "path": "amountUsd", "operator": "exists" }
  ]
}
```

Chain and contract can be auto-filled from your project settings, pass `"useProjectSettings": true` when creating, and blank fields inherit your primary chain and first configured contract.

### Supported chains

EVM: `eth-mainnet`, `base-mainnet`, `arb-mainnet`, `optimism-mainnet`, `matic-mainnet`. Solana: `solana-mainnet`.

Common aliases are normalized for you, `ethereum`, `eth`, and `1` all become `eth-mainnet`; `arbitrum` becomes `arb-mainnet`; `polygon` and `matic` become `matic-mainnet`; `sol` becomes `solana-mainnet`. An empty chain defaults to `eth-mainnet`.

### Event catalog

The platform recognizes 29 event definitions across standards, browsable in the trigger picker when you build an automation.

**EVM**, ERC-20 (`erc20_transfer`, `erc20_approval`), ERC-721 (`erc721_transfer`, `erc721_approval`, `erc721_approval_for_all`), ERC-1155 (`erc1155_transfer_single`, `erc1155_transfer_batch`, `erc1155_approval_for_all`), ERC-4626 (`erc4626_deposit`, `erc4626_withdraw`), governance (`governance_proposal_created`, `governance_vote_cast`), and DeFi (`defi_swap`, `defi_mint`, `defi_burn`, `defi_deposit`, `defi_withdraw`, `defi_borrow`, `defi_repay`, `defi_liquidation`, `defi_claim_rewards`).

**Solana**, `solana_spl_transfer`, `solana_nft_mint`, `solana_nft_transfer`, `solana_dex_swap`, `solana_deposit`, `solana_withdraw`, `solana_anchor_event`.

## Feeding in your own events

For EVM contracts you rarely need this. Send events in yourself only for **Solana contracts** (not auto-watched) or when you **run your own indexer**. Post them through the [Automation Trigger API](/api/automation-triggers), and for product activity rather than on-chain, use [Custom Events](/integrations/custom-events).

When you ingest events yourself, two things help:

- **Set `walletAddress`** at the top level to name the wallet the automation acts on. Otherwise it's inferred from common payload fields (`to`, `owner`, `voter`, `borrower`, and so on).
- **Every `payload` key is filterable** by its path, for example `{ "path": "amountUsd", "operator": "gte", "value": 100 }`.

<Warning>
A malformed contract address is discarded silently rather than erroring, so a typo means the trigger never matches. Preview the trigger before publishing.
</Warning>

## Testing a flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Preview the trigger">
    Preview returns how many wallets match, plus sample profiles. Zero matches means the trigger is wrong, usually the contract address or chain slug.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Do the thing on-chain">
    Perform the activity with a wallet you control, then wait out the finality delay (seconds on an L2, ~2 minutes on Ethereum mainnet) and confirm the automation registered an entry.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm each node fired">
    Check the automation's entries. A `node_skipped` event means the step was reachable but declined to run, suppressed recipients or blocked sending reputation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Widen the audience">
    Only after a single wallet completes the flow correctly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
Don't expect an instant notification when testing. The finality delay means an on-chain trigger fires a short while after the transaction confirms. That's deliberate, so a dropped or re-orged transaction never messages your users.
</Note>

## Best practices

- Scope to a contract address. A chain-wide trigger will match far more than you expect.
- Start from a preset rather than `onchain_event`, the event names and filters are already correct.
- Verify the event mapping with one real transaction before publishing.
- Pair every trigger with a clear outcome; if you can't state what should change, the automation isn't ready.
