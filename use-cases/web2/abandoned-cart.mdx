---
title: "Abandoned Cart Recovery"
description: "Someone starts checkout but doesn't finish, remind them, and recover the sale."
---

**Goal.** When a shopper adds to cart or begins checkout and doesn't complete the purchase, send a timely reminder (and maybe a second) to bring them back.

**Who it's for.** Web2 e-commerce and marketplaces working from email.

**Outcome to move.** Recovered revenue and checkout completion rate.

## Prerequisites

- A [verified sending domain](/settings/domain-authentication) and a [secret key](/integrations/api-keys).
- Consent to email the shopper (from their account or checkout).

## Build it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Send a checkout-started event">
    When a shopper begins checkout, send a `checkout_started` event with the cart in `payload`. Send a matching `purchase_completed` when they finish (see [Purchase confirmation](/use-cases/web2/purchase-confirmation)), the automation uses the gap between the two.

    ```bash
    curl -X POST https://api.onchainsuite.com/api/v1/events \
      -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_live_…" \
      -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
      -d '{
        "event": "checkout_started",
        "contact": { "email": "alice@example.com" },
        "payload": { "cart_id": "C-88", "total_usd": 120.0, "items": 3 },
        "idempotencyKey": "checkout:C-88"
      }'
    ```

    For Python, TypeScript, PHP, or Go, the shape is identical, see the [API Reference](/api-reference) or [Welcome email](/use-cases/web2/welcome-email).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Build the recovery automation">
    Trigger on `checkout_started`, wait, then check whether they bought. The cleanest shape is a [Wait until the contact does something](/automation/create-an-automation#wait): wait for `purchase_completed`, and take the timeout branch if it never comes.

    `trigger (checkout_started) → wait up to 1h for purchase_completed → (timeout) send email (You left something behind)`
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a second nudge (optional)">
    `→ wait 1 day → still no purchase? → send email (Still thinking it over?)`

    Keep it to two. A third reminder rarely converts and costs you unsubscribes.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Activate and publish.">
    Both are required.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Test

Send a `checkout_started` with your own email and no matching `purchase_completed`, then confirm the reminder arrives after the wait. Send the purchase too, and confirm it suppresses the reminder.

## Measure

- **Recovery rate**: reminders that led to a completed purchase.
- **Revenue recovered** versus a holdout.
- **Unsubscribes** on the reminder, if they climb, cut the second nudge.

## Variations

- **Incentive on the second touch.** Free shipping or a small discount can tip a hesitant cart.
- **Segment by cart value.** Treat a big cart differently from a small one.
- **Web3 parallel.** For an on-chain purchase that stalls, see [On-chain purchase follow-up](/use-cases/web3/onchain-purchase).
