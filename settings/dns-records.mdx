title: "DNS Records"
description: "The DNS records that authenticate your sending domain, and what each one does."
---

Authenticating a sending domain adds four kinds of record. The setup flow shows the exact values for your domain — this page explains what each is for.

| Record | Type | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership | TXT | Proves you control the domain before any sending is enabled. |
| SPF | TXT | Lists the infrastructure allowed to send for your domain (`v=spf1 …`). |
| DKIM | **CNAME × 2** | Two records that let outgoing mail carry a cryptographic signature. |
| DMARC | TXT | Tells receiving providers how to treat mail that fails SPF or DKIM. |

<Note>
DKIM is **two CNAME records**, not a TXT record — a common point of confusion. Add both exactly as shown.
</Note>

## Adding them

Enter each record in your DNS provider's zone for the domain, or use the automatic option if your provider is connected. Verification checks the TXT and CNAME (and any A) records and passes once they all resolve.

## If you already send email

You likely already have an SPF and maybe a DMARC record. Don't add a second one:

- **SPF** — a domain may have only one `v=spf1` record. Merge the Onchain Suite include into your existing record; the setup shows the merged value.
- **DMARC** — keep your existing policy; you don't need another record.

The setup detects these and tells you whether to add or merge, so follow what it shows rather than blindly pasting.

## Tips

- Copy each value exactly — no added quotes or trailing characters.
- Make changes in the correct DNS zone.
- Allow propagation time (minutes to an hour, sometimes longer) before re-checking.
- A weak existing setup — SPF `~all`, DMARC `p=none` — still verifies, but the setup flags it so you can tighten it.
