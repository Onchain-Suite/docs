## Platform model

Onchain Suite is designed around what growth teams and developers actually need to do, not around backend services or internal data systems.

An operator team typically works in this order:

1. Create a workspace for your team.
2. Configure a project for a brand, product line, or environment.
3. Verify your sender and domain so email sends from your address.
4. Bring in your audience: import contacts, connect product events, and (for Web3) wallet and contract data.
5. Review audiences, segments, or the first cohort insight.
6. Launch a campaign or automation.

A developer usually starts the other end: send [custom events](/integrations/custom-events) from your product, add the [in-app SDK](/integrations/in-app-notifications) for Web3 push, and let automations react to them.

## Core concepts

### Workspace

A workspace is the top-level home for your organization, billing, and shared access.

### Project

A project contains the settings, senders, integrations, audiences, campaigns, and automations for one operating context.

### Identity: wallet, email, or both

A contact is one record that can be identified by a **wallet**, an **email**, your own **user id**, or all three. Web3 teams usually start from the wallet; Web2 teams from email. None of them requires the others, so a wallet with no email is usable, and so is an email with no wallet.

### Audience

Audiences can come from imported contacts, product events, email engagement, wallet attributes, contract activity, or SQL-generated segments.

### Plays

Plays are pre-built automation patterns for common lifecycle moments: welcome and onboarding, purchases and win-back, plus Web3 moments like first deposit, governance reminders, and migration alerts.

## First-mile goal

The best first experience isn't a big setup project. It's a quick, valuable insight or a small working workflow that proves your data, targeting, and messaging loop are connected.
